# AMOXICILLIN - Penicillin-class Antibiotic
Source: DailyMed (NIH/NLM) - Amoxicillin Capsules/Tablets/Oral Suspension, Initial U.S. Approval 1974
URL: https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=843f6053-63d9-47d2-81f1-b6aa78a7c20c

## INDICATIONS AND USAGE
Penicillin-class antibacterial indicated for infections due to susceptible strains: ear, nose, throat, genitourinary tract, skin/skin structure, lower respiratory tract infections. Also used in combination regimens for H. pylori infection and duodenal ulcer disease (with clarithromycin + a PPI, e.g. lansoprazole).
Should only be used to treat/prevent infections proven or strongly suspected to be bacterial, to limit development of drug-resistant bacteria.

## CONTRAINDICATIONS
- Serious hypersensitivity reaction (e.g., anaphylaxis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome) to amoxicillin or other beta-lactam antibacterials (penicillins, cephalosporins).
- History of allergic reaction to any penicillin.

## WARNINGS AND PRECAUTIONS
- Serious, occasionally fatal hypersensitivity (anaphylactic) reactions reported with penicillin therapy — more frequent with parenteral therapy but has occurred with oral penicillins.
- Drug-Induced Enterocolitis Syndrome (DIES).
- Clostridioides difficile-associated diarrhea (class warning for antibiotics).
- High percentage of mononucleosis patients receiving amoxicillin develop an erythematous rash — avoid use if mono suspected.

## DRUG INTERACTIONS
- **Probenecid**: decreases renal tubular secretion of amoxicillin → increased and prolonged amoxicillin blood levels.
- **Oral anticoagulants (e.g., warfarin)**: appropriate monitoring needed when co-prescribed; anticoagulant dose adjustments may be necessary to maintain desired anticoagulation level.
- **Allopurinol**: concurrent use increases incidence of rash vs. amoxicillin alone (mechanism unclear — may relate to hyperuricemia).
- **Combined oral contraceptives (estrogen/progesterone)**: amoxicillin may affect gut flora, reducing estrogen reabsorption and potentially reducing contraceptive efficacy.
- **Chloramphenicol, macrolides, sulfonamides, tetracyclines**: may interfere with amoxicillin's bactericidal (penicillin) effect in vitro; clinical significance not well documented — theoretical concern with bacteriostatic + bactericidal combination.

## DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION
Adults: 750-1750 mg/day in divided doses every 8-12 hours (dose depends on infection type/severity).
Renal impairment: no dose reduction needed unless severe impairment. GFR <30 mL/min should NOT receive the 875 mg tablet; adjusted regimens required for GFR 10-30 mL/min and dialysis patients.
H. pylori regimen (with lansoprazole/clarithromycin): 1 gram amoxicillin three times daily (q8h) for 14 days.

## ADVERSE REACTIONS
Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, rash; in combination therapy (with lansoprazole), most frequent reported were diarrhea (8%) and headache (7%).

## LABORATORY MONITORING
Periodic assessment of renal, hepatic, hematopoietic function during prolonged therapy. Patients with gonorrhea should have serologic testing for syphilis at diagnosis and follow-up at 3 months.
