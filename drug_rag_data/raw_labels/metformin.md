# METFORMIN HYDROCHLORIDE (Glucophage) - Antidiabetic (Biguanide)
Source: DailyMed (NIH/NLM), setid=857efeaa-0070-4d82-bdee-f7eca1bc0cd9
URL: https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=857efeaa-0070-4d82-bdee-f7eca1bc0cd9

## INDICATIONS AND USAGE
Metformin Hydrochloride Tablets USP is indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycemic control in adults and children with type 2 diabetes mellitus.

## CONTRAINDICATIONS
Contraindicated in patients with:
1. Renal disease or renal dysfunction (serum creatinine >=1.5 mg/dL [males], >=1.4 mg/dL [females] or abnormal creatinine clearance), including from cardiovascular collapse, acute MI, septicemia.
2. Known hypersensitivity to metformin hydrochloride.
3. Acute or chronic metabolic acidosis, including diabetic ketoacidosis.
Should be temporarily discontinued for radiologic studies using intravascular iodinated contrast materials.

## WARNINGS - Lactic Acidosis (Boxed-level concern)
Lactic acidosis is a rare but serious complication, fatal in ~50% of cases when it occurs. Reported incidence ~0.03 cases/1000 patient-years. Risk increases with renal dysfunction, age >=80, hepatic impairment, hypoxic states (CHF, shock, MI), sepsis, dehydration, excessive alcohol intake, and use around IV contrast procedures or surgery. Symptoms: malaise, myalgias, respiratory distress, somnolence, abdominal distress, possible hypothermia/hypotension/bradyarrhythmias.

## DRUG INTERACTIONS
- **Glyburide**: single-dose coadministration did not change metformin PK; glyburide AUC/Cmax decreased variably, clinical significance uncertain.
- **Furosemide**: increased metformin Cmax (+22%) and AUC (+15%); furosemide Cmax/AUC decreased (-31%/-12%), half-life -32%.
- **Nifedipine**: increased metformin Cmax (+20%) and AUC (+9%); enhances metformin absorption; minimal effect on nifedipine.
- **Cationic drugs eliminated by renal tubular secretion** (amiloride, digoxin, morphine, procainamide, quinidine, quinine, ranitidine, triamterene, trimethoprim, vancomycin): theoretical competition for renal tubular transport. Cimetidine specifically shown to increase metformin Cmax +60%, AUC +40% — dose adjustment/monitoring recommended.
- **Hyperglycemia-inducing drugs**: thiazides/diuretics, corticosteroids, phenothiazines, thyroid products, estrogens, oral contraceptives, phenytoin, nicotinic acid, sympathomimetics, calcium channel blockers, isoniazid — may cause loss of glycemic control.
- **Alcohol**: potentiates effect of metformin on lactate metabolism — increases lactic acidosis risk. Patients cautioned against excessive alcohol intake.
- No significant interaction found with propranolol or ibuprofen in single-dose studies.
- Negligible plasma protein binding means low interaction risk with highly protein-bound drugs (salicylates, sulfonamides, chloramphenicol, probenecid).

## DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION
Adults: usual starting dose 500 mg twice daily or 850 mg once daily with meals. Increase by 500 mg/week or 850 mg/2 weeks up to 2000 mg/day; max 2550 mg/day (divided doses, doses above 2000mg better tolerated 3x/day with meals).
Pediatrics (10-16 yrs): start 500 mg twice daily, max 2000 mg/day.
Renal impairment: contraindicated if renal dysfunction present per Contraindications above; use minimum effective dose and monitor renal function regularly, especially in elderly (>=80 requires verified normal creatinine clearance before initiation).

## ADVERSE REACTIONS (>5% incidence vs placebo)
Diarrhea (53.2% vs 11.7%), Nausea/Vomiting (25.5% vs 8.3%), Flatulence (12.1% vs 5.5%), Asthenia, Indigestion, Abdominal discomfort, Headache. Diarrhea led to discontinuation in 6% of patients.

## USE IN SPECIFIC POPULATIONS
Pregnancy Category B (limited human data; insulin generally preferred). Excreted into breast milk in rats (human data lacking) — nursing decision should weigh infant hypoglycemia risk. Not recommended in patients <10 years old.
