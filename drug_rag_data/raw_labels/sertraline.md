# SERTRALINE HYDROCHLORIDE - SSRI Antidepressant
Source: DailyMed (NIH/NLM) - Sertraline Hydrochloride Tablets/Capsules
URL: https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=4e09f580-d612-416c-a4a4-79a4411845b2

## INDICATIONS AND USAGE
Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI) indicated for: Major Depressive Disorder (MDD), Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD), Panic Disorder (PD), Posttraumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD), Social Anxiety Disorder (SAD), Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder (PMDD).

## BOXED WARNING
Suicidal Thoughts and Behaviors — increased risk in children, adolescents, and young adults (up to 24) taking antidepressants; monitor closely especially early in treatment and after dose changes.

## CONTRAINDICATIONS
- **MAOIs**: contraindicated while taking, or within 14 days of stopping, an MAOI (including linezolid and IV methylene blue) — risk of serotonin syndrome. Conversely, at least 14 days must elapse after stopping sertraline before starting an MAOI.
- Do not initiate sertraline in a patient currently being treated with an MAOI such as linezolid or IV methylene blue.
- Hypersensitivity to sertraline or excipients.
- Concomitant use with pimozide is contraindicated (QT prolongation risk, per SSRI class labeling conventions).

## WARNINGS AND PRECAUTIONS - Serotonin Syndrome
Increased risk when co-administered with other serotonergic agents — **but can occur even when sertraline is used alone**. Serotonergic agents of concern: triptans, tricyclic antidepressants, opioids (e.g., fentanyl, tramadol), lithium, tryptophan, buspirone, amphetamines, St. John's Wort, and other drugs impairing serotonin metabolism (MAOIs).
Monitor all patients for emergence of serotonin syndrome (agitation, hallucinations, tachycardia, hyperreflexia, incoordination, nausea/vomiting, diarrhea). Discontinue sertraline and any concomitant serotonergic agent immediately if symptoms occur.

## DRUG INTERACTIONS
- **MAOIs**: contraindicated (serotonin syndrome) — see above.
- **Other serotonergic drugs** (triptans, TCAs, opioids, lithium, tryptophan, buspirone, amphetamines, St. John's Wort): increased serotonin syndrome risk — use with caution, monitor.
- **Bleeding risk**: SSRIs (including sertraline) increase bleeding risk via impaired serotonin-mediated platelet function. Concomitant aspirin, NSAIDs, other antiplatelet drugs, warfarin, and other anticoagulants may compound this risk — case-control/cohort studies show association with GI bleeding.
- **CYP interactions**: sertraline is a moderate CYP2D6 inhibitor, may increase levels of CYP2D6 substrates.
- Prior to initiating, screen patients for personal/family history of bipolar disorder/mania/hypomania (risk of triggering manic episode).

## DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION
Gradually reduce dosage rather than stopping abruptly — discontinuation symptoms can occur. Specific starting doses vary by indication (typically 25-50 mg/day starting dose, titrated based on response, up to 200 mg/day max for most indications).

## ADVERSE REACTIONS (notable, from postmarketing)
Serotonin syndrome, extrapyramidal symptoms (akathisia, dystonia), psychosis, acute renal failure, photosensitivity, severe cutaneous reactions (SJS, TEN), cerebrovascular spasm, vasculitis.

## USE IN SPECIFIC POPULATIONS
Screen for bipolar disorder history before initiating (activation of mania/hypomania risk in antidepressant class).
